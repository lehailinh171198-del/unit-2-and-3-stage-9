2.3 Why Elements React to Form Compounds

Compare Ionic Bonding (electron transfer) vs Covalent Bonding (electron sharing).

Ionic Bonding in NaCl

Sodium loses 1 outer electron to become a positive ion ($Na^+$). Chlorine gains 1 electron to become a negative ion ($Cl^-$).

Summary Rules:
  • Ionic: Metal + Non-metal (Electron Transfer)
  • Covalent: Non-metal + Non-metal (Shared Pair)

3.4 - 3.5 Ways of Transferring Thermal Energy

Conduction in solids, Convection in fluids, and Radiation through vacuum.

Conduction

Particle vibrations passed along adjacent atoms in solids. Metals are excellent conductors due to free delocalised electrons.

Copper > Aluminium > Glass > Wood

Convection

Thermal expansion decreases liquid/gas density, causing warmer fluid to float upwards and cooler fluid to sink.

Examples: Boiling water, sea breezes, air heaters

Radiation

Infrared electromagnetic waves that do not require any particle medium (can travel through a vacuum).

Matte black surfaces absorb/emit best.
Shiny silver surfaces reflect heat.

3.6 Cooling by Evaporation

Demonstrating how high energy liquid particles escape, reducing average kinetic energy and temperature.

Water Gauze 25.0 °C
Ethanol Gauze 25.0 °C

Cambridge Particle Explanation:

Only particles with the highest kinetic energy break intermolecular forces and escape into gas phase.

As high-energy particles leave, the average thermal energy of remaining particles decreases, causing a temperature drop. Ethanol evaporates faster due to weaker intermolecular bonds.
